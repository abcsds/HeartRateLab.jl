01 · The typeface

A monospaced Roman with an even, technical texture

JuliaMono is the community's scientific/technical typeface. Every glyph sits in an identical 600-unit cell, giving the wordmark a steady grid — the same evenness that makes it read as “code”, and the property that turns three capitals into three predictable beats.

03 · Letter widths

Is every letter the same width? Advance yes — ink no

Answer. The advance width is identical — 600 for all twelve glyphs; that is monospace. The ink (actual black) width varies from 469 (H, t) to 516 (r, R), about 10%. So the letters are spaced on a perfect grid but drawn with slightly different amounts of black — which is exactly why the optical centre of a capital is not the same as its cell centre.

06 · Rhythm, the path & the bounce

Three beats, a wrap, and the loop that closes it

The two gaps are H→R = 3008 and R→L = 2416 — ratio 1.25 : 1, exactly Heart (5) : Rate (4).

Constant horizontal speed ⇒ time of flight ∝ distance (a frictionless bounce). So the two long hops genuinely hang longer than the short one: H→R 1504 ms, R→L 1208 ms, wrap 1408 ms. Apex ∝ distance² (height 1.562 : 1). The ball never stops — each contact is a quick elastic squash that launches straight into the next hop.

Does the three-note rhythm have a name? Spoken, HEART·RATE·LAB is three equal stresses — a molossus (— — —). Musically a triplet; the 1.25 spacing makes it a gently swung / dotted triplet. It rhymes with the cardiac cycle the package measures.

The looping path. One ball, four segments, all timed so speed is constant (time ∝ distance): H→R (red), R→L (green), then a wrap — it rises off the right edge and re-enters at the left edge at the same height — carrying it L→H (purple) to start again.

Why the margins matter. The wrap crosses the canvas edges, so the left/right margins set its two spans sr=2000 (L→right) and sl=817 (left→H); together 2817, which fixes the wrap arc's height and duration. Wider margins → a bigger, slower return.

The ball always wears the colour it's about to stamp (anticipation) and deposits its Julia dot on top of the letter, with a margin. The three dots fade out during the wrap, so the loop is seamless.
